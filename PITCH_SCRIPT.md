## ❓ **Q&A PREPARATION**

### **Question 1: "How is this different from Burp Suite or OWASP ZAP?"**

**Answer:**
> "Great question. Burp Suite and ZAP are excellent tools, but they use static wordlists and rule-based detection. They can't adapt to AI-generated attacks that mutate in real-time. CYPHEX uses AI to generate novel payloads on-the-fly, learns from failures, and adapts to the target's specific framework and defenses. Plus, we're a physical device with continuous monitoring, not just a one-shot scanner. Think of it as the difference between a guard dog that learns vs. a security camera that just records."

---

### **Question 2: "Why would anyone trust a device on their network?"**

**Answer:**
> "That's exactly why we built it this way. With cloud scanners, you're sending your code and data to someone else's servers. With CYPHEX, everything runs locally. The AI model runs on the device itself. Your code never leaves your network. Plus, the entire codebase is open-source—you can audit every line. We're not asking for trust; we're giving you transparency."

---

### **Question 3: "What's your moat? What stops someone from copying this?"**

**Answer:**
> "Three things. First, our multi-agent architecture is trained on real attack data from our honeypots—we're constantly learning from actual attackers. Second, we're building a community—500+ GitHub stars, active Discord, pilot customers giving us feedback. Third, we're hardware + software. Copying the code is easy; building a trusted brand around a physical security device takes years. We're not just selling software; we're selling peace of mind."

---

### **Question 4: "How do you handle false positives?"**

**Answer:**
> "False positives are the #1 problem in security scanning. We tackle it three ways. First, our AI agents don't just look for patterns—they verify exploitability. For example, our SQLi agent doesn't just inject a payload; it tries to extract actual data to confirm the vulnerability. Second, we use confidence scoring—each finding is marked as 'confirmed,' 'likely,' or 'possible.' Third, we're building a feedback loop where users can mark false positives, and our AI learns from that. We're not perfect yet, but we're improving every day."

---

### **Question 5: "What about performance? Can a Raspberry Pi really run AI?"**

**Answer:**
> "Yes, but with trade-offs. We use quantized 3B parameter models that run at 5 tokens per second on a Raspberry Pi 5. That's fast enough for analysis and reporting. The actual exploitation—the SQLi, XSS, auth testing—is all I/O-bound, not compute-bound. It's just HTTP requests. So 8 out of 11 agents run at identical speed on IoT hardware. Only the AI analysis agents are slower—2-3 minutes instead of 30 seconds. For users who need faster AI, we offer a Jetson-based version with GPU acceleration. But for most SMBs, the Pi version is perfect."

---

Third, the physical device at $299 one-time for privacy-focused organizations. We're also exploring a managed service for MSSPs who want to deploy CYPHEX for their clients. Year 1 projection is $133K. Year 3 is $1.3M."

Second, direct outreach to SMBs—we're offering free scans to local businesses and converting them to paid customers. Third, partnerships with MSSPs who manage security for multiple clients. We need to build a reliable supply chain for hardware, maintain the open-source community, and provide excellent customer support. We're a small team right now. ### **Question 9: "Why not just use cloud AI instead of edge AI?"**

**Answer:**
> "Privacy and cost. With cloud AI, every scan sends your application data to Cerebras or OpenAI. That's a non-starter for healthcare, finance, and government. With edge AI, everything stays local. Plus, cloud AI costs add up—$0.60 per scan with Cerebras. With edge AI, it's free after you buy the device. We're also targeting IoT and edge deployments where internet connectivity is limited or expensive. Edge AI is the future, and we're positioning ourselves for that."

That's why we're building this."
